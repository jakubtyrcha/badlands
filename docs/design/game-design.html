Game Design Document

Outpost Survival & Ideology

1. Lore & Setting

  • The Setting: An unspecified, aggressively hostile wilderness—a tangled expanse of ancient forests and treacherous swamps. The environment is generic by design to allow for procedural mystery.
  • The Atmosphere: Oppressive and moody. Thick fog rolls through the trees, serving as a stylistic anchor to enhance horror and uncertainty.
  • The Player's Role: You are the ruler/overseer of a frontier colony. Your goal is survival through fulfillment of specific missions (resource extraction, clearing threats, or establishing shrines) that justify the outpost's existence to the Old World.
  • The Old World & The Religion: Colonists originate from a distant, unspecified "Old World." They bring a monotheistic religion centered on Fire and Purity. While the setting is low-fantasy, the religion provides institutional structures: monks, clerics, and inquisitors. The flame is the only truth against the encroaching swamp.

2. Core Gameplay & Autonomous Agents

The player manages the town's infrastructure and economy to influence autonomous heroes who have their own goals and survival instincts.

The Baseline Economy (Core Classes):

These classes are always available and represent the neutral labor of the frontier.

  • Mercenary: Driven by coin. Reliable physical combatants.
  • Grave Robber: Driven by scavenging. Excels at extracting wealth from ruins and the dead.
  • Hunter: Driven by tracking. Vital for scouting. Hunters explore the fog of war to identify incoming threat types, allowing the player to prepare specific defenses.
  • Apprentice: Driven by curiosity. Gathers rare biological and mystical materials.

3. Player Choices & The Castle Core

The Castle levels up as the outpost grows. Each level grants 1 progression point.

3.1 The Ideology Triangle (The Power Curve)

Points are assigned across three psychological approaches to survival.

  • Dogma: The Mind Closed. Survival through absolute obedience to the Flame.
    • Mechanics: Brute strength, heavy armor, high willpower.
    • Curve: Strongest early game. Excels at constant siege and attrition. However, economy scaling is flat. In the late game, it requires highly mindful tactical choices to overcome massive bosses as armor scaling hits diminishing returns.
  • Reason: The Mind Sharpened. Survival through quantification, black powder, and industry.
    • Mechanics: Smart utility, AoE damage, and high DPS.
    • Curve: Weak start as the economy builds. Becomes the ultimate mid-game engine with high options. Lacks "super-powerful" entities late game but has the pockets to solve problems with volume and utility.
  • Instinct: The Mind Surrendered. Survival by embracing the chaotic reality of the swamp.
    • Mechanics: AoE, bizarre utility, and madness mechanics.
    • Curve: The "Carry" faction. Extremely volatile early-game with poor economy and high hero mortality. If it survives to the late game, its unpredictable, shifting mechanics can melt end-game bosses that other factions cannot touch.
Note: Balancing points across the triangle provides adaptability but locks the player out of Tier 3 elite "Capstone" buffs and exclusive class variants.

3.2 The Guilds (Unlocks at Castle Level 2)

The player invites one exclusive Guild to establish a monopoly (e.g., Blacksmiths for gear, Apothecaries for healing, Powder-Makers for ballistics).

3.3 The Orders (Unlocks at Castle Level 2)

The player invites one exclusive Religious/Militant Order.

  • The Institutional Order: Focus on Inquisitors and state control (Counter-Reformation style).
  • The Radical Order: Focus on Witch Hunters and populist zeal (Reformation style).

4. Threat Taxonomy

The wilderness reacts to the settlement. Scouting via Hunters is mandatory to determine frequency and type.

4.1 Threat Archetypes

  • Swarms: Rats, bandit raids, low-tier wildlife. Constant pressure.
  • Goliaths: Mud-golems, apex beasts, lumbering giants. Single-point massive damage.
  • The Esoteric: Ghosts, shadows, dryads, sirens, and harpies. Immaterial or mind-altering.
  • The Corrupted: Cultists and rogue human fanatics.
  • The Blight: Plagues and biological rot that cripples the agent economy.
  • The Undead: Necromancers reanimating fallen heroes and Grave Robbers.
  • Chaos Entities: Twisted, deformed creations and "Biblical" horrors (demons/angels of burning eyes and wheels).
  • Environmental: The fog itself, causing heroes to go mad or become lost.

4.2 Apex Bosses

  • The Old Vampire Lord: Aristocratic horror in the deep woods.
  • The Lake Monster: A leviathan that emerges as the town nears water.
  • The Fallen Angel: A "wheels within wheels" construct of fire and bronze.
  • The Black Cube: A void manifesting as an inversion of light; alters physics/geometry.
  • The Mad Acolyte: A former colonist leading a hyper-buffed army of fanatics.

5. Balancing & The Player Dilemma

5.1 The Synergy vs. Complementary Dilemma

The strategy lies in the Synergy vs. Complementary Dilemma.

The Strategic Choice:

  • Synergy: A Dogma player chooses the Sword Order to double down on melee power (Overkill, but safe against physical sieges).
  • Complementary: A Dogma player chooses the Apothecary Guild to patch their weak late-game economy and provide AoE plague damage to handle swarms.

Timing: Committing to a solution early allows for deeper development, while being reactive (scouting and waiting to choose) keeps options open but leaves the town vulnerable during the "decision gap."

5.2 Class Balancing (Core Classes)

Each class is rated across three attribute groups — Utility (healing, debuff, buff, economy, gameplay), Damage (ranged, dps, aoe, armour-piercing, ethereal), and Survival (armour, evasion, hitpoints, willpower).

Ratings describe a representative late/mid-game hero (level 15); actual values vary during the game. All core classes are "basic": none will be exceptional — exceptional ratings are reserved for exclusive classes.

Economy notes: the Grave Robber recycles graves, corpses, and salvage back into the economy; the Hunter contributes through skinning. The Hunter's gameplay utility reflects exploration — scouting the fog of war; the Apprentice's reflects teleportation. The Apprentice's ranged rating comes from ranged spells; the Mercenary's debuff is a shield bash (stunning).

5.3 Power Curve Balancing

Game phases: the game splits into early, mid, late, and late+:

  • Early — castle level 0/1
  • Mid — castle level 1/2 (castle level 1 spans the late-early → early-mid transition)
  • Late — castle level 3
  • Late+ — when heroes get beyond level 20

Hero levels: heroes level from 1 to 20+:

  • Level 1–8 — early
  • Level 9–15 — mid
  • Level 16–20 — late
  • Level 20+ — late+ (the exp needed to increment rises dramatically)

Threat level: a number used to balance and calibrate combat (exact values are work-in-progress):

  • Something analogous to the Elo rating; used to calibrate mechanics against intended anchor points.
  • Exact interpretation is an open question.
    • Invariant: threats of the same caliber should have more-or-less a 50% chance of winning against each other (on average, taking all threats of the same level into account — strengths and weaknesses might significantly shift balance for a specific pair).
    • Open question: how does a difference in threat level shift the expected win ratio?
    • Open question: how does one-vs-many calibration work?
  • Example anchor values:
    • Rat — threat 0.25. Designed as a threat to civilians, but can threaten early heroes up close and in larger numbers.
    • Bandit — threat 2. A decent challenge early game, or later in larger numbers.
    • Bandit leader — threat 5. A serious challenge early game.
    • Ghost — threat 8. A serious challenge early game.

6. Classes

"Power" (player's minions) and "threat" (enemies) are interchangeable for the intended power curves below.

6.1 Mercenary

  • Design rationale: Tank and early/mid game melee backbone.
  • Backstory: High-rank criminal running away from justice, petty noble with gambling debts, or a seasoned veteran from a disbanded army.
  • Skills: Shield bash (lvl 3)
  • Price: 450

6.2 Hunter

  • Design rationale: Ranged backbone and utility engine.
  • Backstory: Poacher escaping justice.
  • Skills: Dress wounds (lvl 2), Skin game (lvl 1)
  • Price: 350

6.3 Grave Robber

  • Design rationale: Flexible backbone with utility.
  • Backstory: Robbed too many graves.
  • Skills: Rob grave (lvl 1), Sneak (lvl 3), Precision shot (lvl 5), Backstab (lvl 3)
  • Price: 300

6.4 Apprentice

  • Design rationale: Carry — expensive and fragile, but very powerful when it survives long enough.
  • Backstory: Student of arcana, caught cheating or escaping gambling debts.
  • Skills: Spells (a variation of skills, no mechanical difference at the moment): Curse (lvl 1), Calcify (lvl 4), Teleport (lvl 8)
  • Price: 600

  • Recruited at: Monastery (requires 1 Dogma)

7. Buildings

7.1 Non-buildable

  • Castle — single entity, the starting point of the outpost. Its destruction is a loss condition; fortified.
  • House — economy; urban sprawl.
  • Sewer — indestructible; urban sprawl.

7.2 Utility

  • Watchtower — fortified. Price 500.
  • Apothecary — provides potions for heroes to buy. Requires Castle level 2. Price 700.
  • Blacksmith — requires Castle level 2. Price 600.
  • Marketplace — price 500.
  • Inn — price 300.
  • Shrine — requires 1 Dogma.
  • Temple — requires 2 Dogma.
  • Brothel — requires Thieves' Den level 2.
  • Gambler's Den — requires Thieves' Den level 3.
The Shrine/Temple ladder is Dogma's; equivalent Reason and Instinct building ladders are work-in-progress.

8. Economy

  • Buildings generate taxable income by passive increment + economic activity.
  • Heroes' income is not taxed, but their spending generates economic activity.
  • The tax collector spawns at the castle gate and transfers taxable gold from visited buildings.
  • The tax collector needs to return to the castle/watchtower to transfer the gold to the player's coffer; if there's no way to cover the distance during the 10am–4pm hours, multiple tax collectors spawn.
  • Tax collectors will be priority targets.
